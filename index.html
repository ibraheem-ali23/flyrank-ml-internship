Machine Learning Capstone Content Refresh & Discoverability

Prioritizing Content Refresh Candidates Using Search & Staleness Signals

A privacy-safe, decision-support ranking framework comparing a transparent rule-based baseline against a tabular ML model on anonymized search intelligence data.

Title & Abstract

Editorial teams with constrained weekly capacity often struggle to decide which existing pages should be refreshed first when organic search momentum slows. We frame content refresh prioritization as a supervised risk-scoring and ranking problem over anonymized page-level search and freshness signals using pre-decision inputs only. We evaluate a transparent weighted baseline score alongside a supervised classification model while enforcing strict leakage and privacy controls. Directional validation indicates that combining content staleness with visibility and engagement indicators surfaces higher-priority refresh candidates than unranked heuristics or single-threshold rules. The resulting ranked queue provides practical decision support for editorial triage while remaining bounded by observational data limits.

Introduction & Problem Statement

Publishing new articles alone is rarely sufficient to sustain organic search visibility over time; older pages may lose relevance, ranking position, or click appeal as search intent and SERP competition evolve. Because content teams have finite bandwidth per sprint, reviewing pages at random or relying on a single static cutoff can waste editorial effort on low-impact pages or miss high-visibility pages showing early decay signs.

Decision Supported: Rank existing pages by directional refresh priority—paired with clear reason codes and suggested actions—so editors can focus review time on high-opportunity candidates first.

Data

This work uses the anonymized search intelligence dataset slice for the Content Refresh & Discoverability lane (data/raw/content_refresh_anonymized.csv and warehouse slice conventions across observation windows such as month=2026-03, keeping final/holdout slices separated during evaluation).

  • Unit of Analysis: One row represents a single anonymized page record over a defined observation window, paired with pre-decision search and content attributes.
  • Excluded Columns & Leakage Prevention: Direct outcome derivatives and post-window or label-derived fields (such as raw percentage trend changes or label copies) are excluded from model inputs to prevent target leakage.
  • Privacy & Safety Guardrails: No client names, raw domains/URLs, private search queries, credentials, or raw data dumps are exposed anywhere in this report or repository.

Methodology

We structured the workflow progressively from task framing (w02_ml_task_framing.ipynb) and data contract verification (w03_data_contract.ipynb) to baseline scoring and top-queue inspection (w04_baseline_score.ipynb), followed by supervised modeling comparison.

Target Proxy
Binary Decline Flag

Derived from observed downward trend direction (trend_direction == 'down') as a directional proxy—not causal ground truth.

Core Pre-Decision Signals
5–6 Features

Freshness/age indicators (days_since_last_update, content_age_days), search exposure/engagement (impressions_90d, avg_position, ctr), and content length.

Baseline & Validation Design

  • Rule-Based Baseline: Computes a normalized composite score over staleness and visibility/engagement signals, assigning interpretable reason codes and action labels without using label-derived columns.
  • Validation & Leakage Checks: Uses holdout/grouped evaluation principles and explicit checks confirming zero overlap between input features and label-derived columns.

Results

Across directional evaluation slices, combining multi-signal ranking with supervised modeling improves top-K prioritization over simple unranked or single-rule baselines while keeping outputs transparent and auditable.

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Directional & Decision-Support Only: Observed metrics and model scores indicate relative refresh priority; they do not prove causal impact of a content edit or reveal proprietary search engine ranking algorithms.
  • Proxy Label & Window Constraints: Trend labels summarize recent historical movement over fixed windows and may miss seasonal cycles, delayed recovery, or external SERP shifts.
  • Heuristic vs. Non-Linear Trade-offs: Linear or threshold rules are easy to audit but can over-rank static evergreen pages or miss nuanced feature interactions.
